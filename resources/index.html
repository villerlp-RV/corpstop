Resources for small water systems

The rule applies to every community water system, but most of the guidance, help and money is built for systems with small staff. These are the official resources we'd start with. They're free, and none of them are LineTally's.

Start here: what the rule requires

  • Lead and Copper Rule Improvements (EPA)Web

    EPA's main LCRI page: overview, key dates and links to everything else.

  • Fact Sheet: Small Water SystemsPDF

    What the rule means for small systems, in plain language. The best first read.

  • Fact Sheet: Service Line Inventory and Replacement RequirementsPDF

    Inventory, replacement plan and replacement requirements in one place.

  • Fact Sheet: Calculating Service Line ReplacementsPDF

    How the replacement pool and the required pace are worked out.

  • Fact Sheet: Deferred Deadlines for Service Line ReplacementPDF

    When a system can take longer than 10 years, and what it has to document.

  • Fact Sheet: Inventory ValidationPDF

    Checking that non-lead lines in the inventory really are non-lead.

  • Fact Sheet: Public Education RequirementsPDF

    What customers must be told, and when.

  • Rule Comparison Guide for Public Water Systems and Primacy AgenciesPDF

    Side-by-side comparison of the earlier rule and the LCRI.

  • The final rule in the Federal Register (89 FR 86418)Web

    The full rule as published on October 30, 2024, with EPA's explanation.

  • 40 CFR Part 141, Subpart I (eCFR)Web

    The current regulation text. Section 141.84 covers inventories, replacement plans and replacement.

Building your service line inventory

  • Developing and Maintaining a Service Line Inventory: Small Entity Compliance GuidePDF

    EPA's inventory guide written for small systems.

  • Guidance for Developing and Maintaining a Service Line InventoryPDF

    EPA's full inventory guidance: records review, field methods and categorizing lines.

  • EPA Inventory TemplateExcel

    EPA's spreadsheet template. Check whether your state requires its own format instead.

Free technical assistance

  • Your state rural water association (National Rural Water Association)Web

    State associations and their circuit riders give small systems hands-on help, often at no cost.

  • Rural Community Assistance Partnership (RCAP)Web

    Regional partners that provide technical assistance to small and rural water systems.

  • EPA Water Technical AssistanceWeb

    EPA-funded help for communities with planning, funding applications and projects.

  • Lead Service Line Replacement Accelerators (EPA)Web

    EPA's program helping communities plan lead service line replacement and apply for funding.

Funding

  • Drinking Water State Revolving Fund (DWSRF)Web

    Low-cost loans and, for some systems, principal forgiveness, run through each state. Lead service line replacement is an eligible use. Contact your state's DWSRF program.

  • USDA Rural Development: Water & Waste Disposal Loan & Grant ProgramWeb

    Loans and grants for water systems in rural areas.

Your state primacy agency

  • Association of State Drinking Water Administrators (ASDWA)Web

    Find your state's drinking water program. Your state sets the inventory format, reviews your replacement plan and decides on deferred deadlines.

For your customers

  • Protect Your Tap: A Quick Check for Lead (EPA)Web

    A step-by-step guide residents can use to check their own service line. Useful for customer-submitted photos.

  • Lead (EPA)Web

    EPA's general information on lead and health, to point customers to.
